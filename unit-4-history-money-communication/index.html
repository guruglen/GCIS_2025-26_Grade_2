Grade 2 Newsletter: Through Time and Trade

Upcoming Unit: History, Money and Communication

Dear Parents,

We are excited to embark on a fascinating journey through time! This unit will explore how people lived, worked, and communicated in the past, while also understanding the important role of money and trade in our communities.

Central Idea:
"Human systems for trade and communication emerge as solutions to shifting needs and historical contexts."

🏛️ Humanities: People, Money and Trade

Students will explore the economic and social aspects of communities:

  • Money Recognition: Identifying national currency and understanding what people use it for in daily life.
  • Jobs and Income: Exploring the connections between money and jobs, and how people earn money in their local area.
  • Financial Literacy: Analyzing how people spend money, why some people don't have enough to meet their needs, and how and why people save money.
  • Community Support: Understanding why people help others in their communities.

📚 Past: Economy and Communications

We'll travel back in time to discover:

  • Daily Life: Learning what people ate and where their food came from in historical contexts.
  • Markets and Trade: Exploring what could be bought at markets and how people traded for goods and services.
  • Written Communication: Discovering forms of written communication from the past.
  • Message Delivery: Understanding how messages were delivered between distant places before electronic communication.

📖 English Language: Reading and Writing

Our literacy focus will develop comprehension and creative skills:

  • Reading Comprehension: Understanding implicit meaning in texts, predicting story endings, identifying settings and characters from different times and places, and making simple inferences about feelings.
  • Literary Analysis: Commenting on writers' use of language, structure, and vocabulary choices like adjectives.
  • Biographical Structure: Understanding how biographies are organized with chronological events, significant life achievements, and historical context, and exploring poetry with focus on words, sounds, rhyme, and rhythm.

✍️ English Language: Writing and Speaking

Students will develop their expression and communication skills:

  • Writing Skills: Selecting appropriate content and language for different genres and audiences, beginning to use dialogue in biographical writing, developing biographies with historical settings, key figures, and significant life events.
  • Sentence Structure: Using various sentence structures and punctuation accurately, finding alternatives to "and/then," using past and present tenses, varying sentence openings, and organizing ideas in paragraphs.
  • Speaking and Listening: Extending experiences and ideas through role-play activities.

🔢 Mathematics: Number, Geometry and Statistics

Our mathematical learning will connect to real-world applications:

  • Money: Recognizing value and money notation in local currency, and comparing values of different coin and note combinations.
  • Time: Ordering and comparing units of time (seconds to years), reading time to five minutes in digital and analog formats, and interpreting calendar information.
  • Probability: Using language associated with patterns and randomness, conducting chance experiments with two outcomes, and presenting results.

🕰️ Learning Activities: An Immersive Journey Through Time

Students are acting as "Time Travelers" to solve historical problems across three distinct eras of Taiwan's history:

Era 1: The Age of Barter (Pre-1600s)

Students take on the role of Siraya villagers in the Tainan plains. They must solve the problem of Resource Scarcity—trading surplus deer hides for essential iron tools and salt—using "Silent Barter" and non-verbal communication with foreign merchants.

Era 2: The Port of Anping (1800s)

Students navigate the complexity of a global port city. They explore the shift from bartering to Early Currency (Silver Dollars) and the challenge of communicating trade deals between local farmers and British tea merchants.

Era 3: The Economic Miracle (1950s–Present)

Students step into the industrial rise of Taiwan. They investigate how the community moved from subsistence farming to high-tech investment, exploring how Modern Communication and the New Taiwan Dollar fueled the "Silicon Shield."

Literacy & Inquiry Integration

Throughout the unit, students maintain a Time Traveler's Journal. They use Tier 2 vocabulary (like negotiate, exchange, and invest) to document their solutions, write persuasive arguments for "Needs vs. Wants," and draft narratives from the perspective of historical problem-solvers.

🎯 Summative Assessment: The "Tainan Problem-Solver" Biography

For the final project, students will research and write a Biography of a significant figure in Taiwan's history (e.g., Koxinga, Yoichi Hatta, or K.T. Li).

The Task

Students must identify a specific problem their subject faced regarding trade, money, or communication and explain how that person's solution helped the community thrive.

The Product

A written biography and a "Living Museum" presentation where students dress up as their subject and share their story with the school community.
